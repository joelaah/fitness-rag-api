## Flexibility, Mobility, and Warm-Up Protocols

Flexibility refers to the passive range of motion of a joint, while mobility refers to the ability to actively control movement through a range of motion under load. Both are essential for safe, effective strength training and injury prevention.

### Dynamic Warm-Up (Pre-Workout)

A proper warm-up increases core body temperature by 1-2 degrees Celsius, increases blood flow to working muscles, improves nerve conduction velocity, and reduces injury risk by up to 50%. Dynamic stretching is superior to static stretching before exercise.

**General Warm-Up Protocol (5-10 minutes)**:
1. Light cardio: 3-5 minutes of brisk walking, cycling, or jumping jacks to elevate heart rate.
2. Joint circles: 10 circles each direction for ankles, knees, hips, shoulders, wrists.
3. Dynamic stretches specific to the training session.

**Lower Body Dynamic Warm-Up**:
- Leg swings (front-to-back): 10-15 per leg. Opens hip flexors and hamstrings.
- Leg swings (side-to-side): 10-15 per leg. Opens hip adductors and abductors.
- Walking lunges: 10 per leg. Activates quads, glutes, hip flexors.
- Bodyweight squats: 15-20 reps. Full range of motion, controlled tempo.
- Glute bridges: 15 reps with 2-second hold. Activates the gluteus maximus.
- Inchworms: 5-8 reps. Warms up hamstrings, core, and shoulders simultaneously.

**Upper Body Dynamic Warm-Up**:
- Arm circles: 15 each direction (small to large). Warms up shoulder joint.
- Band pull-aparts: 15-20 reps. Activates rear deltoids and rhomboids.
- Scapular push-ups: 10-15 reps. Engages the serratus anterior.
- Cat-cow stretches: 10 reps. Mobilizes the thoracic spine.
- Wall slides: 10-15 reps. Improves overhead mobility and scapular control.

**Exercise-Specific Warm-Up Sets**: Before each compound lift, perform 2-4 progressive warm-up sets:
- Set 1: 40% working weight × 10 reps
- Set 2: 60% working weight × 5 reps
- Set 3: 80% working weight × 3 reps
- Set 4: 90% working weight × 1 rep (optional, for heavy sessions)
Rest 60-90 seconds between warm-up sets.

### Static Stretching (Post-Workout)

Static stretching is best performed after training when muscles are warm and pliable. Hold each stretch for 30-60 seconds. Do not bounce. Breathe deeply and relax into each stretch.

**Key Static Stretches**:
- Hip flexor stretch (kneeling lunge): 30-60 seconds per side. Critical for anyone who sits for extended periods.
- Hamstring stretch (standing or seated forward fold): 30-60 seconds. Essential after deadlifts and squats.
- Chest and anterior shoulder stretch (doorway stretch): 30-60 seconds per side. Counters rounded shoulder posture.
- Lat stretch (hanging from a bar or using a doorway): 30 seconds per side.
- Piriformis stretch (figure-four): 30-60 seconds per side. Relieves hip tightness and sciatic tension.
- Calf stretch (wall lean): 30 seconds per leg.

### Mobility Drills for Common Limitations

**Limited Ankle Dorsiflexion** (affects squat depth):
- Wall ankle stretch: Face a wall, place one foot 3-5 inches from the wall. Drive the knee forward over the toes without lifting the heel. Hold 30 seconds, 3 sets per ankle.
- Banded ankle distraction: Attach a resistance band around the front of the ankle, step back to create tension. Perform knee drives over the toes. 10-15 reps per side.

**Limited Hip Mobility** (affects squat, deadlift):
- 90/90 hip stretch: Sit with front leg at 90 degrees, back leg at 90 degrees. Lean forward over the front shin. Hold 30-60 seconds per side.
- Deep squat hold (goblet position): Hold a light kettlebell or dumbbell at chest height, sink into a full squat, and hold for 30-60 seconds. Push knees out with elbows.
- Pigeon pose: 30-60 seconds per side. Targets external hip rotation.

**Limited Thoracic Extension** (affects overhead press, bench press):
- Foam roller thoracic extension: Lie on a foam roller positioned at mid-back. Arms behind the head. Extend backward over the roller for 3-5 seconds, then curl up. 10-15 reps, moving the roller up and down the thoracic spine.
- Open book stretch: Lie on your side with knees bent. Rotate the top arm open like a book, following with your eyes. Hold 3-5 seconds. 10 reps per side.

**Limited Shoulder Mobility** (affects overhead press):
- Wall slides: Stand with back against a wall. Arms at 90 degrees (goal post position). Slide arms up the wall while maintaining contact with elbows, wrists, and back. 10-15 reps.
- Shoulder dislocations with PVC pipe or band: Grip wide, slowly rotate the pipe overhead and behind the body. 10-15 reps. Progressively narrow the grip over weeks.

### When NOT to Stretch

- Never perform aggressive static stretching on a cold muscle (risk of strain).
- Avoid static stretching immediately before maximal strength or power efforts — it can temporarily reduce force production by 5-8% for up to 30 minutes.
- If a stretch causes sharp, acute pain (not gentle tension), stop immediately. Pain may indicate an injury, not a flexibility limitation.
