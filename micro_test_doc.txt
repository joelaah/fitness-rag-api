Project Apollo: Mission Overview
The Apollo program was the third United States human spaceflight program carried out by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA). It succeeded in landing the first humans on the Moon in 1969.

Flight Crew of Apollo 11
The crew consisted of Neil Armstrong as mission commander, Michael Collins as command module pilot, and Buzz Aldrin as lunar module pilot. Armstrong and Aldrin landed the Apollo Lunar Module Eagle on July 20, 1969.

Lunar Surface Activities
Neil Armstrong became the first person to step onto the lunar surface six hours and 39 minutes later, on July 21 at 02:56 UTC. Aldrin joined him 19 minutes later. They spent about two and a quarter hours together outside the spacecraft and collected 47.5 pounds (21.5 kg) of lunar material.

Splashdown and Recovery
Apollo 11 ended on July 24, 1969, with splashdown in the Pacific Ocean, approximately 812 nautical miles southwest of Hawaii. The crew was recovered by the USS Hornet.
